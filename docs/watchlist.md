# Watch list: Complete Feature Guide

**Part of Phase 1 of the ABM Intelligence Platform. The holding pen that keeps Discovery
high-signal.**

In one line: the Watch list is where weak leads wait. A company showing only a faint sign of
intent (a single routine billing or coding hire and nothing stronger) is kept out of the
main Discovery channel so that channel stays sharp, but it is not thrown away. It is parked
here, watched on a clock, and automatically promoted back to Discovery the moment it heats
up.

This is the panel that makes the rest of the system trustworthy. Because the weak leads have
somewhere to go, the Discovery panel can stay a tight list of companies genuinely worth
acting on, and because nothing is discarded, a company that starts as a faint signal and
turns into a real opportunity is never missed.

*Audience: marketing and leadership. No technical background needed.*

## 1. Why it exists

A target list is only valuable if it is sharp. If every faint signal landed in Discovery,
the panel would fill with companies that posted a single routine billing job, and the team
would spend its time wading through noise to find the few that matter.

But the opposite mistake is just as costly: if those faint signals were simply discarded, we
would lose companies that are quietly on their way to becoming real opportunities. The
hospital that posts one coder today might post three more and hire a new revenue-cycle
director next month. That is exactly the company we want, caught early.

The Watch list resolves the tension. Weak leads are pulled out of Discovery so the main
channel stays high-signal, but they are held here, watched continuously, and promoted back
the instant they strengthen. Nothing clutters the workspace, and nothing falls through the
cracks.

---

## 2. What counts as a weak lead

The platform tracks many buying signals (new executives, funding, layoffs, acquisitions,
engagement, and revenue-cycle hiring). Most of these are strong on their own. The one that is
often just noise is a single, routine hire.

Revenue-cycle job postings come in two grades:

- **Core roles** (prior authorization, denials, eligibility, claims, revenue cycle, and
  similar) are the high-value work we automate directly. A single one is a real buying
  signal and goes straight to Discovery.
- **Standard roles** (billers, coders, patient access, scheduling) are higher-volume and
  noisier. A single open standard role is usually just routine backfill, not a sign the
  company is rethinking how it runs.

So the rule is simple: a company whose only signal is **one standard role, and nothing
stronger**, is a weak lead. It goes to the Watch list instead of Discovery. The moment it
has anything more (a second role, or any other signal), it is no longer weak.

---

## 3. The two kinds of watched company

The Watch list has two sections, because a weak lead can reach it by two different routes.

### Parked, not yet qualified

These are companies the system found, saw only a single standard role on, and deliberately
**did not spend money researching**. Confirming whether a company truly fits our ideal
customer profile uses paid research, and it is not worth paying that for a faint, likely-
routine signal. So the company is parked: noted, watched, and left un-researched until it is
worth the spend.

Each parked entry shows the lightweight provenance: the open role, the location, when it was
first parked, and a link to the sample job posting. They are re-checked on every scan, so the
moment one opens a second role, it is researched and qualified automatically.

### Qualified, low intent

These are companies the system **already researched and confirmed are a real fit**, but whose
buying intent is currently weak (again, a lone standard role and nothing stronger). They are
genuine ideal-customer accounts, just quiet right now. Rather than let them dilute the active
Discovery list, they are held here, with the same readout a Discovery row would have (segment,
ABM match, signals, and an intent meter showing the Watch tier), and a countdown showing when
they will move if nothing changes.

The difference in one line: **parked** companies have not cost us anything yet, while
**qualified, low-intent** companies are confirmed fits waiting for their moment.

---

## 4. Automatic promotion

The whole point of the Watch list is that it is not a graveyard. A watched company graduates
back to Discovery automatically, with no one having to check, the moment either of these
happens:

1. **A second revenue-cycle role opens.** One standard role is routine; two is a deliberate
   build-out. The company now clears the bar and is researched and moved into Discovery on
   the next scan.
2. **Any stronger signal lands.** A new executive, a funding round, an acquisition, a core
   role, or engagement with us all override the weak-lead rule immediately.

For the qualified-but-low-intent leads, "heating up" also means their intent score crossing
back into the active range. Either way, promotion is hands-off: the company simply reappears
in Discovery, fully researched, ranked by its new, stronger intent.

---

## 5. The clock: TTL and pruning

Every watched company sits under a time limit (a TTL, or time to live), so the list stays
honest and never grows stale.

| Kind | The clock |
|------|-----------|
| **Parked** | If a parked company is not seen again for about 30 days (it stopped hiring, or we stopped finding that posting), it is pruned from the watch ledger. The list self-corrects to only currently-active situations. |
| **Qualified, low intent** | These follow the same self-cleaning lifecycle as Discovery: if they stay cold, they age toward review and eventually auto-reject, and they are always restorable. A countdown on each row shows when the next move will happen. |

The result is a watch list that reflects what is actually happening in the market right now,
not a pile of months-old postings.

---

## 6. The confirmed-ABM exception

There is one deliberate override. If a weak-lead company is a **confirmed target on our ABM
list**, it is always shown in Discovery, never hidden on the Watch list, even if its only
signal is a single routine hire.

The reasoning: a company we have already chosen to pursue matters regardless of how strong
this week's hiring signal is. We never want a tracked account quietly tucked away in the
holding pen. So the Watch list filters out weak leads, with a confirmed ABM target as the one
thing that always earns a place in the main view.

## 7. The Watch list screen

The screen is titled "Watch list" with a running count of how many companies are watched, and
the line: "Low-intent leads, a single standard RCM role (biller, coder) and nothing stronger.
Kept out of Discovery so it stays high-signal, watched here with a TTL, and auto-promoted the
moment a second role opens or a real signal lands."

![The Watch list](./images/watch_01_top.png)

The two sections appear stacked, each with its own count:

- **Qualified, low intent** (when present): the confirmed fits that are currently quiet.
- **Parked, not yet qualified**: the un-researched single-role companies.

When there is nothing to watch, the screen says so plainly: low-intent hires collect here
instead of cluttering Discovery. The page is intentionally read-only. There are no buttons to
push, because promotion is automatic. It is a window into what the system is holding, not a
place that needs managing.

## 8. Anatomy of a watched row

The two sections show slightly different rows, matched to how much we know about each company.

### A parked row

| Element | What it tells you |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Company name** | The organization. |
| **Role tag** | "1 open standard role" or, if more accumulate, the count of standard postings. |
| **Roles and location** | The role bucket (for example Coder or Biller) and the city and state. |
| **Parked time** | How long ago it was first parked, for example "parked 3d ago". |
| **Sample posting** | The actual job title, linking to the original posting. |

### A qualified, low-intent row

| Element | What it tells you |
|---------|-------------------|
| **Company name** | The organization. |
| **Segment badge** | Health System, Specialty, or Payer. |
| **ABM badge** | Whether it matches our ABM target list. |
| **Signal chips** | The buying signals on it (the lone standard role). |
| **Countdown** | When it will auto-move if nothing changes. |
| **Evaluated date** | When it was qualified. |
| **Intent meter** | The intent score and the Watch tier. |

## 9. The "watching N" strip in Discovery

The Watch list is not hidden away. On the Discovery panel, a subtle line reminds the team how
much is being held in reserve, for example "Watching 67 companies with a single open
revenue-cycle role; they auto-qualify the moment a second role opens."

This is reassurance, in the workspace where it matters: the team can trust that the tight
Discovery list is tight on purpose, and that a large, well-managed reserve is being watched in
the background and will surface anything that strengthens.

---

## 10. The cost angle

The Watch list is also a cost-control mechanism, and a significant one.

Confirming whether a company fits our ideal customer profile uses paid research. The parking
rule means we never pay that cost for a faint, likely-routine signal: a single standard role
is parked for free and only researched if it stacks into something real. Across a market full
of routine billing and coding postings, that is the difference between paying to research
every one and paying to research only the companies showing a genuine build-out.

In short, the Watch list lets the system cast a wide net (noticing every faint signal) while
spending money only on the signals that prove themselves.

- [x] Pulls weak leads (a lone standard revenue-cycle role, nothing stronger) out of the main Discovery channel

- [x] Parked, not yet qualified: single-role companies we deliberately have not paid to research
- [x] Qualified, low intent: confirmed ideal-customer fits that are currently quiet
- [x] Each shown with the right level of detail (provenance for parked, full readout for qualified)

**Automatic promotion**
- [x] A second revenue-cycle role opening promotes the company back to Discovery
- [x] Any stronger signal (new exec, funding, acquisition, core role, engagement) promotes it immediately
- [x] Promotion is hands-off: the company reappears in Discovery, fully researched and re-ranked

- [x] Parked companies pruned after about 30 days unseen, so the list self-corrects
- [x] Qualified low-intent leads follow the self-cleaning Discovery lifecycle, and are restorable
- [x] A per-row countdown shows when the next move will happen

**Exceptions and visibility**
- [x] Confirmed ABM targets are always shown in Discovery, never hidden here
- [x] A "watching N" strip on the Discovery panel keeps the reserve visible

- [x] Wide coverage of every signal, with spend only on signals that prove themselves
